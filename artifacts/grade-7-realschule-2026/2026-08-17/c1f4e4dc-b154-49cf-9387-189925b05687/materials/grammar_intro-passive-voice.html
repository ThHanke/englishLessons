Passive Voice — be + past participle

In a passive sentence, the thing the action happens to comes first, then a form of the verb "be" plus the -ed/-en form of the main verb (the same form used in "has cleaned"). We use it when who does the action is unknown, unimportant, or already obvious. German forms this in a similar way ("wird gereinigt" = a helping verb plus the same past-participle form), so this pattern will feel familiar — the main difference is the helping verb itself (German werden, English be).

  • The caretaker cleans the classroom every morning.
    The classroom is cleaned every morning.
  • The school gives new books to every student.
    New books are given to every student.
  • The form teacher explains the timetable.
    The timetable is explained by the form teacher.
