Passive Voice — be + past participle

In a normal sentence, the subject does the action: "The director films the scene." In a passive sentence, we put the thing that receives the action first: "The scene is filmed." You do not have to say who does the action. How to form it: [thing] + is/are + the -ed/-en form of the verb. This -ed/-en form is the past participle — the same form you already know from regular verbs in the simple past: film → filmed, produce → produced, edit → edited. Use is for one thing, are for more than one thing: "The show is broadcast every week." "The scenes are edited by the director." If you want to say who does the action, add by: "The scene is filmed by the director." German note: In German you say "Der Film wird gedreht" — with werden. In English, use be (is/are), NOT become. Do not say: "The film becomes filmed." Say: "The film is filmed."

  • They produce the show in Berlin.
    The show is produced in Berlin.
  • The director edits the scenes.
    The scenes are edited by the director.
  • The studio broadcasts the news every evening.
    The news is broadcast every evening.
  • They film the documentary in London.
    The documentary is filmed in London.
