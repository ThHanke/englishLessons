Simple Present of 'be' — am, is, are (recap)

The verb 'be' has three forms in English: 'am' (only for 'I'), 'are' (for 'you'), and 'is' (for 'he', 'she', 'it', and names like Tom or Anna). German has a similar pattern — bin, bist, ist — but the English forms do not match one-to-one, so you cannot translate directly. The most common mistake: writing 'I is' because 'er ist' feels similar. There is no 'I is' in English. 'I am' is always the right form for 'I'. In questions, the form of 'be' comes before the subject: 'Are you...?' not 'You are...?' Watch out also for 'your' (dein/deine) — it is not the same as 'you' (du).

  • I is ten years old.
    I am ten years old.
  • She am from England.
    She is from England.
  • How old you are?
    How old are you?
  • What is you name?
    What is your name?
