Simple Present of 'be' — am, is, are

In English, the verb "be" changes depending on who you talk about. Use "am" with "I", "are" with "you", and "is" with "he", "she", or a name. Good news: just like in German ("Bist du?"), English questions swap the verb and the subject — "Are you?" It works the same way! To say something is not true, add "not" after the verb: "I am not from France." One important difference from German: in English, only names of people and places start with a capital letter. The word "name" stays lowercase: "My name is Anna." not "My Name is Anna."

  • I am ten years old.
  • You are Anna.
  • He is Max.
  • I am Anna.
    Are you Anna?
  • I am from France.
    I am not from France.
