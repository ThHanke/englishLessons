Prepositions for Time — at, in, on

English uses three small words to say when something happens: at, in, and on. Each one goes with a different type of time expression — they are not interchangeable. Use at with clock times: at seven o'clock, at half past nine. Use in with parts of the day (morning, afternoon, evening) and seasons: in the morning, in winter. Use on with days of the week: on Monday, on Friday. Watch out: German am looks like "at the" in English, but the English rule for parts of the day is in, not at. So am Morgen = in the morning (not "at the morning"). German am Montag = on Monday (not "at Monday"). German um 7 Uhr = at 7 o'clock.

  • um 7 Uhr (German)
    at 7 o'clock
  • am Morgen (German)
    in the morning
  • am Montag (German)
    on Monday
  • am Nachmittag (German)
    in the afternoon
  • at half past nine — at school — on Friday — in the evening
